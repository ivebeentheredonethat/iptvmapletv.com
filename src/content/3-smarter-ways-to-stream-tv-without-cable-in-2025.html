Cable bills keep climbing, and most households watch only a handful of the channels they pay for. If you want live TV, sports and movies without a cable contract, there are three realistic routes in Canada. Each has a different price, a different catalogue and a different catch.

Option 1: licensed streaming apps, one subscription each

Services such as Crave, CBC Gem, Sportsnet+, TSN+ and Netflix are official, simple and stable. The catch is that each one covers only part of what you watch. Hockey may be on one app, football on another and your favourite series on a third, so the monthly total grows with every service you add. They are a good fit if you watch two or three things and want everything fully licensed in Canada.

Option 2: a live-TV bundle from a telecom or an app

Telecom TV packages delivered over the internet, and some bundle-style apps, put many channels in one guide with a DVR. They feel closest to cable, but they often come with a contract, equipment fees and a smaller selection of international channels. Check the total after fees, not the advertised price.

Option 3: an IPTV subscription

An IPTV subscription puts live channels, sports and an on-demand library in one app, on the devices you already own. How to decide

  1. Write down what you actually watch each week: which sports, which channels, which series.
  2. Add up what you pay today, including equipment rental and fees.
  3. Price out the same viewing with one or two apps, and compare it to a single IPTV plan.
  4. Test before you commit. Our free trial costs nothing, and the 7-day money-back policy covers a paid plan.
